Car Condos in Florida: 2026 Market Guide

November 7, 2026 · AutomotiveCondos.com

Florida is the center of gravity for the American car condo market. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory currently tracks 99 car condo and garage condo communities in Florida, more than any other state, with 4,149 known units across the 81 projects that have published a unit count. That makes Florida the largest state in our directory by a wide margin, and it reflects a simple reality: Florida combines a huge population of collectors and retirees, year-round driving weather, no state income tax, and a long list of homeowners associations that do not allow a car, boat or RV to sit in the driveway.

This guide is written for buyers searching for car condos in Florida, garage condos in Naples, or a private "man cave" in Tampa, Sarasota, Jacksonville or the Palm Beaches. It walks through where the inventory actually sits, what each region costs, which projects are selling now versus planned, and the Florida-specific issues (hurricanes, flood zones, insurance and HOA rules) that should shape your purchase. Every count and price below comes from the full Florida directory, which you can also browse on the interactive map.

Florida Car Condos at a Glance

Before we break the market down region by region, here are the headline numbers from the AutomotiveCondos.com directory as of November 2026:

  • 99 communities across 46 Florida cities and towns.
  • 4,149 known units among the 81 communities that disclose a unit count (18 projects have not published one yet, so the true total is higher).
  • 36 communities are actively selling, 26 are resale only (sold out from the developer), 12 are under construction and 25 are planned.
  • 35 communities publish pricing, with starting prices ranging from about $175K to $1.1M.
  • Southwest Florida alone holds 38 communities and 1,834 known units, about 44% of every known unit in the state.

Put differently: if you are shopping for a car condo in Florida, you have more choice than anywhere else in the country, but that choice is heavily concentrated on the Gulf Coast between Sarasota and Naples. Buyers elsewhere in the state often have only a handful of options, which makes timing and pre-construction reservations more important.

Southwest Florida: Naples, Fort Myers and Charlotte County

Southwest Florida is the most developed car condo market in the United States. The region's 38 communities in the directory stretch from Marco Island north through Naples, Bonita Springs, Estero, Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Punta Gorda, Rotonda West and Englewood.

Naples leads the state with 12 communities and 743 known units. If you are researching car condos in Naples, you will find the full range: seven projects actively selling (including Jetport Motor Suites, Prima Auto Condos, Naples Prestige Auto Suites, Ultimate Garages and The Hideout Elite Motor Storage), one under construction (Naples Auto Vault), one resale-only building (Naples Motor Condos on Livingston Rd) and three planned projects. The planned list includes the largest single project in our Florida data, the Hyper Club / Naples Racing Resort, with 400 units listed. Naples also sits at the luxury end of the price spectrum: The Hideout lists units at $650K to $699K and Naples Luxury Motor Suites lists around $930K.

The depth of the Naples market is not an accident. Collier County is home to the Revs Institute, one of the most important automotive collections in the country, and the area's seasonal population includes a large number of serious collectors who split time between the Northeast or Midwest and Southwest Florida. For those owners, a climate-controlled garage condo solves a real problem: a place to keep (and enjoy) cars that will not fit in a gated-community garage.

Fort Myers is the volume market. The directory lists 9 Fort Myers communities with 515 known units, and six of them are selling now. Vehicle Fortress is the largest at 170 units with listed pricing of $182K to $370K, and Toy Vault Condo has 96 units from $175K to $500K. Mid-market projects such as Excalibur Motor Condominiums ($374K to $395K), Lakeside Storage Suites ($390K to $410K) and Classic Ultra Storage Condos ($425K to $489K) cluster tightly in the high $300Ks to the $400Ks. Miami, Broward and the Panhandle

Miami-Dade and Broward have surprisingly few car condos for their population, largely because industrial land is scarce and expensive. CollectionSuites Miami in Doral (38 units, from $1.1M) has the highest starting price of any selling project in the state, Auto Vault Ft Lauderdale has 84 units and is selling, and Revault (Miami) and Pembroke Pines Collector Car Condo are planned. In the Panhandle, Garage 30A in Santa Rosa Beach (52 units) is under construction and two other projects near Destin and DeFuniak Springs are planned.

Selling, Resale, Under Construction or Planned: What Status Means for You

Florida's 99 communities split into four statuses in the directory, and each one implies a different buying strategy.

  • Selling (36 communities, 1,486 known units). The developer still has inventory. You can usually choose a unit position, sometimes customize the mezzanine or finishes, and negotiate directly with the sales team. Ask how many units remain and whether pricing steps up as the project sells through.
  • Resale only (26 communities, 1,325 known units). The building is sold out and units change hands owner to owner. You get a finished, insured, operating association with a track record of dues and assessments, which removes a lot of risk. The trade-off is limited selection: a resale-only building may have one or two listings at a time, or none.
  • Under construction (12 communities, 305 known units). Typically sold with a deposit and a contract that closes at completion. Pricing is often the best you will see on a new project, but you are underwriting the builder's schedule.
  • Planned (25 communities, 1,033 known units). Projects still in entitlement or pre-sales. Some will open on time, some will change scope, and some may never break ground. Reservation deposits should be refundable and held in escrow.

One-quarter of Florida's known units are in planned projects, so the statewide supply picture could look very different in two to three years. Florida-Specific Issues Every Car Condo Buyer Should Check

Hurricane-rated construction

A car condo is a box with a very large door, and in a hurricane the overhead door is often the weakest point of the building. Florida's statewide building code includes wind-load requirements for doors and openings, and the strictest provisions apply in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone that covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties. When you tour a building anywhere in the state, ask for:

  • The wind-load and impact rating of the overhead doors and any storefront glass (and the Florida product approval number).
  • The roof system, its age and how it is attached.
  • Whether the building has a generator or backup power for gates, lighting and dehumidification after a storm.
  • How the association handled the most recent named storm: any claims, assessments or downtime.

Southwest Florida buyers have a recent reference point. Hurricane Ian made landfall in Lee County in September 2022, and many owners in Fort Myers, Cape Coral and Punta Gorda learned exactly how their buildings and associations performed. Ask the board or manager directly.

Flood zones and elevation

Wind is only half the risk. Storm surge and freshwater flooding can destroy a collection even when the building stays intact. Check the FEMA flood zone of the parcel, the finished floor elevation of the units, and whether the association carries flood coverage on the structure. A ground-floor bay that sits a few inches above grade in a coastal zone is a very different asset from one built on an elevated pad inland. In practice, many owners keep a written storm plan: what gets moved to an upper mezzanine, which cars go on lifts, and who has keys if the owner is out of state.

Insurance

Florida's property insurance market has been under real strain in recent years, and that flows directly into car condo HOA dues because the association insures the building. Ask for the master policy declarations page, the windstorm deductible (often a percentage of insured value) and the last two years of budget increases. Separately, insure your vehicles with a collector car or agreed-value policy that covers storage at a garage condo, and insure your interior build-out (lifts, cabinets, flooring, bar) under a unit-owner policy. Our cost guide walks through typical line items.

HOA and use rules

Florida car condos are usually governed by condominium declarations under state condo law, and the use restrictions vary widely. Some buildings allow lounges, kitchenettes and social gatherings; some prohibit overnight stays outright; many restrict business use, vehicle repair or fuel storage. If you want to run a hobby workshop, store an RV or boat, or host clients, read the declaration and rules before you sign.

Is a Florida Car Condo a Good Investment?

Many Florida buyers start with a use case (they need a place for a collection) and only later ask whether the unit will hold its value. A few Florida-specific factors help the investment case: deep demand from collectors and seasonal residents, strict HOA rules in master-planned communities that push vehicles, boats and RVs out of driveways, and limited industrial land in coastal counties. Against that, you have rising insurance costs, a large pipeline of planned supply in some metros, and illiquidity: resale-only buildings may only see a handful of trades per year.

The resale prices in our directory give some evidence of a functioning secondary market. Tampa's 300-unit Motor Enclave trades from the high $300Ks, Sarasota's Luxe Dream Garage trades near $510K, and Riviera Beach's The Hangar trades near $950K and up. How to Shop for a Car Condo in Florida

  1. Pick a region before a project. Most owners visit their unit weekly, so a 20-minute drive matters more than a fancy lounge. Use the interactive map to see what is near your home or seasonal residence.
  2. Decide on size. A single collector car needs far less space than a lift-stacked collection, a motorcoach or a boat on a trailer. Confirm door height and width, clear ceiling height for lifts, and whether a mezzanine is included or optional.
  3. Compare status and timing. Selling projects offer choice, resale buildings offer certainty, and planned projects offer early pricing.
  4. Read the documents. Budget, reserve study, insurance and use restrictions. In Florida, those four documents tell you most of what you need to know.
  5. Model the full cost. Purchase price plus HOA dues, property tax, insurance, utilities and build-out. The cost guide has a worksheet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many car condo communities are there in Florida?

The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 99 car condo and garage condo communities in Florida as of November 2026, with 4,149 known units across the 81 projects that publish a unit count. Of those, 36 are selling, 26 are resale only, 12 are under construction and 25 are planned.

Where are most car condos in Florida located?

Southwest Florida. The region from Naples and Marco Island north to Englewood holds 38 of the state's 99 communities and about 44% of known units. Naples leads all cities with 12 communities, followed by Fort Myers (9), Sarasota (7), Jacksonville (6) and Punta Gorda (6).

How much does a car condo cost in Florida?

Among the 35 Florida communities that publish pricing, starting prices range from about $175K to $1.1M. Ten communities start under $250K, 13 start between $250K and $399K, six start between $400K and $599K, and six start at $600K or more. Charlotte County and Fort Myers are the most affordable areas, while Naples, Palm Beach County and Miami are the most expensive.

How much do garage condos cost in Naples, FL?

Naples sits at the upper end of the Florida market. In the directory, The Hideout Elite Motor Storage lists units at $650K to $699K and Naples Luxury Motor Suites lists around $930K, while several other Naples projects price on inquiry. Nearby Bonita Springs and Fort Myers offer lower entry points, with many Fort Myers units listed in the high $300Ks to $400Ks.

Do Florida car condos need hurricane protection?

Yes. Ask for the wind and impact rating of the overhead doors and glass, the roof system, the FEMA flood zone and floor elevation, and the association's master insurance policy and windstorm deductible. Buildings in Miami-Dade and Broward must meet the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone provisions of the Florida Building Code, and buyers elsewhere in the state should hold projects to a similarly high standard.
