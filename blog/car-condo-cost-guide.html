How Much Does a Car Condo Cost in 2026?

November 6, 2026 · AutomotiveCondos.com

Car condo pricing is wide: entry-level units in secondary markets start under $200K, typical units in strong markets run $300K–$600K, and finished luxury vaults in premium communities trade from $800K past $2M. Here's how to read the range.

What drives the price

  • Market. Florida is the deepest car condo market in the country, and within it, metro matters: Tampa Bay and Southeast Florida command far more than inland secondary markets. Track-side communities (units attached to a motorsports circuit) carry their own premium.
  • Size. Units commonly run 600 to 2,500+ sq ft. Price per square foot is the honest comparison — most of the market falls roughly between $175 and $325/sq ft, with premium markets well above that.
  • Door and ceiling height. A 14-foot door that clears a Class C motorhome — or 16 feet for a Class A — adds real money. So does the clear height for a four-post lift.
  • Finish level. A bare shell and a finished unit with mezzanine, bath, A/C, and epoxy are different products. Finished resales often price near what the build-out actually cost — sometimes less.
  • Phase. Pre-construction pricing in an early phase is usually the cheapest a community ever sells for. The trade: you don't control the delivery date.

New vs. resale

New units from the developer typically price above comparable resales — you're paying for current construction costs and first choice of location in the building. Resales between owners can be the better deal, especially when the seller already invested in the build-out.

The ongoing costs nobody mentions

  • Association dues — usually modest ($100–$400/month is common), covering the shell, common areas, and master insurance.
  • Property taxes — it's deeded real estate; budget for your county's rate on the assessed value.
  • Insurance on contents and interior improvements — the association's policy covers the structure, not your cars or your build-out.
  • Utilities — power for the lift and A/C is on you.

Bottom line

Figure the real comparison in $/sq ft against other communities in the same metro, then adjust for door height, finish, and dues. Compare communities in your state — and if you own a unit and wonder what it's worth today, talk to us.
