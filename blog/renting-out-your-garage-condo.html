Renting Out a Garage Condo: Rates, Rules & Real Returns

November 6, 2026 · AutomotiveCondos.com

The quiet secret of the garage condo world: a lot of owners never park a car in their unit. They lease it — to the collector who isn't ready to buy, the small business that needs deeded-quality space without buying, the RV owner whose HOA just sent another nastygram. Done right, it's some of the lowest-drama rental income in real estate.

What units actually lease for

Rates track size, finish, and market. Rough bands we see across the country: basic unfinished bays in secondary markets from a few hundred dollars a month; mid-size finished units in active markets $800–$1,500; large, climate-controlled units with mezzanines in premium metros $1,500–$2,500+. One data point from our directory: a 29-unit Spokane community leases units from about $1,250/month. Your true comp is whatever similar units in your own metro lease for — check the communities near you on our map and call around.

Read the declaration first

Before you buy with rental intent, confirm three things in the condo documents:

  • Leasing rules. Minimum lease terms? Board approval? A cap on the percentage of units leased at once?
  • Use rules. Can a tenant run a business? Store a commercial vehicle? Wrench on cars? The answer varies wildly between communities.
  • Insurance requirements. Most associations require owners (and often tenants) to carry their own liability and contents coverage.

The underwriting math

Say a unit costs $350K with $250/month dues, $300/month taxes, and $75/month insurance. At $1,400/month rent you're grossing 4.8% and netting roughly 2.6% before any debt — plus whatever appreciation the market gives you. That's not a cap-rate monster; it's a hard asset that pays its own carry while it (historically, in good markets) appreciates. Buyers using leverage need the rent to clear the commercial loan payment too — see our financing guide.

Finding tenants

Demand usually finds you: community waiting lists, the association's own grapevine, local car club groups, and RV/boat owner forums. Good tenants stay years — moving a car collection is a project nobody repeats casually.

What can go wrong

The short list: a tenant running an unauthorized business that violates zoning; uninsured contents losses becoming your problem; associations tightening lease rules after you buy (get the current rules in writing); and vacancy in thin markets. None of these are exotic — they're the standard landlord homework, minus the toilets.

Thinking of buying a unit to lease? Compare communities and pricing in your state, or if you already own one and want it in front of buyers instead, list it with us.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a garage condo rent for?

Depending on market, size, and finish level, garage condo leases commonly run from a few hundred dollars a month for basic units to $1,000–$2,500+ for large finished units in premium markets. Local comparables matter more than national averages.

Can my HOA stop me from renting my garage condo?

Yes — the condo declaration governs. Some associations allow leasing freely, some require minimum lease terms or board approval, and some prohibit commercial activity by tenants. Read the declaration before you buy with rental plans.

Is garage condo rental income passive?

Mostly. With one tenant, no plumbing in many units, and the association maintaining the shell, garage condos are among the lowest-touch rentals in real estate — but you still handle leasing, insurance, and the occasional tenant issue.
