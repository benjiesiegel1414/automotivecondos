Car Condos in California: 2026 Market Guide

November 7, 2026 · AutomotiveCondos.com

California has the deepest car culture in the country and some of the thinnest car condo inventory. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 21 car condo and garage condo communities in California, with 465 known units among the 10 projects that publish a unit count. For comparison, Florida has 99 communities in the same directory. The gap is not about demand: Southern California alone is home to an enormous population of collectors, builders, racers and enthusiasts. It is about land. Industrial and flex parcels in coastal California are expensive, heavily entitled and fought over by logistics, life science and housing uses, so very few developers can make a collector garage project pencil.

The result is a market that looks very different from the rest of the country. Most California communities are sold out, many trade only on resale, and the new projects that do come to market are priced like luxury real estate. This guide covers where the 21 communities are, what they cost, which ones you can actually buy into today, and how California-specific issues (land costs, Prop 13 property tax, seismic design and insurance) affect ownership. Every figure comes from the full California directory, which you can also explore on the interactive map.

California Car Condos at a Glance

  • 21 communities across 19 cities, from Napa and El Dorado Hills in the north to San Diego and the Coachella Valley in the south.
  • 465 known units across the 10 communities that disclose unit counts. Eleven communities, including several established resale markets, do not publish a count.
  • Only 3 communities are actively selling from the developer. Eight are resale only, 4 are established clubs or developments that trade privately, 2 are under construction and 4 are planned.
  • Published starting prices run from the $300Ks to $1.4M, and the top of the published ranges reaches $3.5M.
  • The Coachella Valley has the most communities (6), and the planned Caves project in Murrieta is the largest single project by units (120).

If you are searching for car condos in California, the most important thing to understand is that availability is the constraint, not price. In most of the state, you will be shopping resale listings, joining a waitlist or reserving a unit in a project that has not opened yet.

Coachella Valley: Palm Desert, Palm Springs, Indio and Thermal

The desert is California's most active car condo region, and it is the one place in the state where land economics look a little more like Florida or Arizona. The directory lists six communities here. GarageTown Palm Desert trades on the resale market, and the Finish Line Auto Club brand has a planned Palm Desert location; compare both on the car condos in Palm Desert page. MonSTORE Garages has locations in Palm Springs and Coachella, and Easy Street Garages is in Indio. All three of those trade through resales and rentals, which makes the Coachella Valley one of the few California markets where you can rent a garage condo before you buy one.

The valley's anchor is The Thermal Club, a private motorsport country club in Thermal with trackside villas and garages. It is in a different category from a typical garage condo: owners are buying into a members-only driving club with its own road course, and the garage is part of a residential and track lifestyle rather than a standalone storage unit. The club has also put the region on the map nationally by hosting high-profile events, including an IndyCar exhibition race in 2024. For collectors who want a place to actually drive their cars, not just store them, Thermal is the reference point.

The Coachella Valley also benefits from the same seasonal pattern that drives Southwest Florida: a large population of part-time residents who own homes in HOA-governed communities and need somewhere to keep cars, motorcycles, golf carts and toys.

Los Angeles and Ventura County

Five communities sit in Los Angeles and Ventura counties, and the pricing tells the story of Southern California land costs. Finish Line Auto Storage in Westlake Village is one of only three selling projects in the state, with 30 units listed at $606K to $1.2M. A sister Finish Line project in Calabasas (30 units) is planned and awaiting approval. In Newbury Park (Thousand Oaks), Lavery Court Car Condos has 31 units that trade on resale around $775K. The South Bay gets its first big luxury project with Grand Prix Auto Club Torrance, which is under construction with 33 units priced at $1.4M to $3.2M. Out in Santa Paula, Chaparral AeroLofts (37 units, planned) combines the car condo concept with aviation.

Orange County, San Diego and the Inland Empire

Finish Line Auto Club in Costa Mesa is one of the most established luxury garage communities in the state, with 32 units and a published range of $750K to $3.5M. Grand Prix Auto Club San Diego is under construction with 23 units at $1.25M to $3.37M. These two projects show what a coastal Southern California car condo costs when it is built new: well above a million dollars for a typical unit.

The Inland Empire offers the opposite end of the market. The Caves in Murrieta is planned with 120 units, the largest project in the California directory, and it lists pricing from the $300s. Eucalyptus at Beaumont is an established development with deeded RV and garage lots. If you live in Riverside County or north San Diego County and want a car condo without coastal pricing, this is where to look.

Monterey, Santa Cruz and the Bay Area

Monterey is the spiritual home of the California collector car world. Every August, Monterey Car Week brings auctions, tours, track events at WeatherTech Raceway Laguna Seca and the Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance to the peninsula; the 2026 edition ran August 7 to 16 and marked the Concours's 75th year. That makes Monterey one of the most sought-after places in the country to own a garage. The directory lists two communities here: Garage Unlimited of Monterey, which is sold out and trades only on resale, and Monterey Motorsport Park, an 86-unit community that also trades on resale. Browse both on the car condos in Monterey page.

Nearby, Garage Unlimited of Santa Cruz trades on resale around $475K. In San Jose, Club Auto Sport is an established Silicon Valley collector car club with private garages that trade on resale. Bay Area buyers should expect very limited inventory and should register interest with each community in advance.

Napa and the Sierra Foothills

Northern California's two remaining communities are both selling. Napa Vault Garage Condos is selling in wine country (pricing on inquiry), and Hobby Condos El Dorado Hills, east of Sacramento, has 43 units priced from about $350 per square foot. For Sacramento-area collectors, El Dorado Hills is one of the few places in the state where you can buy new from a developer at a price per square foot that looks more like the national market.

Five of those seven communities start at $600K or more. In Florida, by contrast, only six of 35 priced communities start that high. A few takeaways:

  • Coastal new construction is a seven-figure product. Both Grand Prix Auto Club projects start above $1.2M, and the Costa Mesa range tops out at $3.5M.
  • Resale is the value play in established markets. Santa Cruz around $475K and Newbury Park around $775K are well below new-build pricing in comparable coastal areas.
  • Inland is where sub-$500K options live. The Caves in Murrieta (from the $300s) and Hobby Condos El Dorado Hills (from about $350 per square foot) are the clearest entry points.
  • The desert is a rental and resale market. Three Coachella Valley communities trade through resales and rentals, so expect pricing on request rather than a published sheet.

Why California Has So Few Car Condos

California's shortage of car condos comes down to three forces that rarely line up for a developer.

Land and entitlement costs

Car condos are typically built on industrial or light industrial land. In coastal California, that land is among the most expensive in the country, and it competes with warehouse, last-mile logistics and research uses that can generate higher returns per acre. On top of land cost, entitlement in many California cities is slow and uncertain. Our directory's Calabasas listing is a good example: a 30-unit project that is still awaiting approval. Long timelines add carrying cost, which ends up in the sale price.

Construction costs

California building and energy codes, seismic design requirements, labor costs and impact fees all push construction cost per square foot above the national norm. A simple tilt-up or pre-engineered metal building that works in Fort Myers or Quincy, Washington, can cost meaningfully more to build in Torrance or San Diego. That is a big reason the new projects in the directory (Grand Prix Auto Club in both cities) are positioned as luxury clubhouses with seven-figure pricing rather than basic storage units.

A sold-out existing stock

Several of California's best-known communities, such as Garage Unlimited of Monterey, Monterey Motorsport Park and Lavery Court, are sold out from the developer. With so little new supply, owners tend to hold, and resale listings can be sparse. Eight of the 21 communities in the directory are resale only and four more are established communities that trade privately, so more than half of the California market is not available from a developer at all.

California-Specific Ownership Issues

Property tax under Prop 13

California property tax is based on the purchase price, not a market value that resets every year. Under Proposition 13, the base rate is 1% of assessed value plus any local voter-approved assessments, and the assessed value can rise by no more than 2% a year until the property is sold. For a car condo buyer, that means your property tax is largely locked in at purchase, which helps with long-term budgeting, but it also means a buyer paying today's prices will pay more tax than a neighbor who bought years ago. Ask the seller or developer about Mello-Roos or other special assessments, which are common on newer projects.

Seismic considerations

California is earthquake country. If you plan to install vehicle lifts or tall storage racks, confirm that the slab thickness and anchoring meet the lift manufacturer's requirements, and ask the association whether lifts need to be engineered or approved. Store fluids and batteries properly, and secure shelving to walls.

Insurance

California's property insurance market has tightened in recent years, particularly in wildfire-exposed areas. The association's master policy covers the building, and its cost flows into HOA dues, so ask for the policy declarations and recent premium history. Separately, insure your vehicles with an agreed-value collector policy that covers storage at the garage condo, and insure your interior improvements under a unit-owner policy.

Use rules and the club model

California's high-end communities often operate more like private clubs than storage buildings, with lounges, events and concierge services. Finish Line, Grand Prix Auto Club and The Thermal Club all lean into this model. Read the declaration carefully for rules on overnight use, business use, guests and events, and understand what portion of your dues funds staff and programming.

Is a California Car Condo a Good Investment?

The case for California car condos rests on scarcity. With only 21 communities statewide, very limited new supply and a huge collector population, existing units in Monterey, Orange County and Los Angeles face very little competing new supply. Resale levels in the directory, such as roughly $775K at Lavery Court and roughly $475K in Santa Cruz, show that owners can find buyers at substantial prices.

The risks are cost and liquidity. Seven-figure new builds need a lot of appreciation to outperform, dues at club-style communities can be high, and thin resale markets can take time to sell. Most California buyers are best served by treating the unit primarily as a use asset (a place to store, work on and enjoy cars) with appreciation as a possible bonus. Our investment guide covers appreciation, rental income and exit planning in more depth.

How to Buy a Car Condo in California

  • Get on lists early. In sold-out communities, the best units are often sold to people who registered interest with the association, manager or a local broker. Contact each community you are interested in.
  • Consider reservations on planned projects. Four California communities are planned and two are under construction. Make sure deposits are refundable and held in escrow until closing.
  • Look inland if price matters. Murrieta, Beaumont, El Dorado Hills and the Coachella Valley offer far lower entry points than the coast.
  • Try before you buy in the desert. Several Coachella Valley communities offer rentals, which let you test the location and the association before committing.
  • Read the documents. Budget, reserves, insurance, use restrictions and any special assessments. Our buying guide explains what to look for.
  • Model total cost. Purchase price plus Prop 13 property tax, dues, insurance, utilities and build-out. Use the cost guide to plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many car condo communities are there in California?

The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 21 car condo and garage condo communities in California as of November 2026, with 465 known units across the 10 projects that publish unit counts. Three are selling, eight are resale only, four are established, two are under construction and four are planned.

How much does a car condo cost in California?

Published starting prices in the directory range from the $300s (The Caves in Murrieta) to $1.4M (Grand Prix Auto Club Torrance), and the top of published ranges reaches $3.5M at Finish Line Auto Club in Costa Mesa. Five of the seven communities with published starting prices begin at $600K or more, so California is far more expensive than the national market.

Where can I buy a car condo in California right now?

Only three California communities in the directory are selling from the developer: Finish Line Auto Storage in Westlake Village, Hobby Condos El Dorado Hills and Napa Vault Garage Condos. Two more (the Grand Prix Auto Club projects in Torrance and San Diego) are under construction, and most other communities trade through resales, private sales or rentals.

Are there car condos near Monterey and Pebble Beach?

Yes. The directory lists Garage Unlimited of Monterey and Monterey Motorsport Park (86 units), both of which trade on resale only. Garage Unlimited of Santa Cruz is also nearby, with resales around $475K. Inventory is limited, especially around Monterey Car Week in August, so register interest with each community early.

Why are car condos so expensive in California?

California car condos are built on scarce, expensive industrial land, face long entitlement timelines, and carry higher construction costs from seismic and energy codes, labor and impact fees. Combined with a large collector population and a mostly sold-out existing stock, that pushes coastal new builds into seven-figure territory. Inland areas such as Murrieta, Beaumont, El Dorado Hills and the Coachella Valley are more affordable.
