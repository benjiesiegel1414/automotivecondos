Ohio is quietly one of the largest car condo and storage condo markets in the country. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 25 communities in Ohio with 2,360 known units across the 21 projects that publish a unit count. That unit total is larger than many states with far bigger populations, and it comes from two very different engines: a mature boat and RV storage condo market on the Lake Erie shore, and a newer wave of luxury car condo projects around Columbus, Cincinnati and Cleveland.

That split shapes everything about buying here. On the lake, you can still find resale units in the $60Ks to $100Ks in large, established associations. In the suburbs, new purpose-built motor suites are priced from the $300Ks to $550K. This guide covers where Ohio's 25 communities are, what they cost, which are selling now, and what to check before you buy. All figures come from the full Ohio directory, which you can also browse on the interactive map.

Ohio Car Condos at a Glance

  • 25 communities across 20 cities, concentrated in four regions: the Lake Erie shore, Northeast Ohio, Central Ohio and Southwest Ohio.
  • 2,360 known units across 21 communities that publish counts. Four (Torque Motor Suites Oakley, Park Place Storage Condominiums in Lebanon, Marysville Executive Storage Condos and Biz Caves) do not publish a count.
  • 11 communities are selling from the developer, 7 trade on resale only, 2 are established and 5 are planned.
  • Published prices run from about $66K (resales at Catawba Storage Condominiums) to $550K (the top of the planned range at The Motor Enclave New Albany).
  • Catawba Storage Condominiums (I-VI) in Port Clinton is the largest community at 379 units, followed by Store-With-Us Storage Condominiums in Medina at 340.

If you are searching for car condos in Ohio, decide first which market you are shopping in. A lakefront boat storage condo and a climate-controlled motor suite with a lounge mezzanine are both called "condos," but they are different products with different buyers, prices and HOA cultures.

The Lake Erie Shore: Port Clinton, Marblehead and Catawba Island (7 communities, 882 units)

The Lake Erie shore in Ottawa County is the single biggest storage condo cluster in Ohio, with about 37% of the state's known units. This is the jumping-off point for the Lake Erie islands (Put-in-Bay, Kelleys Island and Middle Bass), the Marblehead Peninsula and Catawba Island, and it is home to a huge seasonal boating community. Owners need somewhere secure to keep boats, trailers, golf carts and cars between weekends, and indoor storage condos have filled that need for years.

Port Clinton alone has four communities and 555 known units. Catawba Storage Condominiums (I-VI) is the largest community in the state at 379 units, with resales around $66K to $194K and a sixth phase under construction. NorthCoast Storage Condominium (96 units, resales about $70K to $100K) and West Bay Storage Condominiums (48 units, resales about $80K) are resale-only, while Island Landings Business & Storage Condos is selling 32 units at about $180K. On the peninsula, Safe Harbor Storage Condos in Marblehead is selling 185 units with 2026 sales from $90K to $330K, one of the widest ranges in Ohio. In Lakeside Marblehead, Shoreline Storage Suites is selling 56 units with 2026 sales from $140K to $200K, and East Harbor Condominium Storage trades on resale only, with 86 units and resales around $97K to $134K. Because this market is mature, it offers something rare: real price discovery. Several hundred units means frequent resales, established HOAs and plenty of comparable sales. The trade-off is that many units were designed for boats first and cars second, so check door sizes, floor finish, power and whether heat is allowed if you want to use the space as a car condo.

Northeast Ohio: Cleveland, Akron and Medina (8 communities, 761 units)

Greater Cleveland has eight communities. Medina is the heavyweight: Store-With-Us Storage Condominiums is a 340-unit, resale-only project, the second largest in Ohio, and Biz Caves is planned. See all car condos in Medina.

West of Cleveland, Avon Storage Condos is selling 62 units from about $120K, Avon Lake Office & Storage Condos trades on resale only (90 units, $85K to $195K), and Buckeye Storage Condominiums in Columbia Station is resale only with 40 units around $75K. East and south of the city, Tin Man Storage Center in Mentor is selling 113 units, Mega Storage Condominiums in Twinsburg is selling 107 units, and Hudson Pointe in Hudson is selling 9 units at $216K to $263K.

Northeast Ohio is a practical market. Most projects are flexible storage condos that serve collectors, boat owners, contractors and small businesses side by side, and pricing is accessible compared with the new luxury suites farther south.

Central Ohio: Columbus Suburbs (5 communities, 624 units)

The Columbus area is where Ohio's car condo market is growing fastest, especially in the affluent northern suburbs. The marquee project is Resolute Motorsports Club in Delaware, a planned 250-unit pre-construction project. The Motor Enclave New Albany is planned at 165 units with expected pricing from $160K to $550K, and Marysville Executive Storage Condos is also planned.

Two Columbus-area communities are already operating. Horsepower Farms in Powell is selling 92 units from $156K to $290K, and Executive Storage Estates in Plain City trades on resale only, with 117 units and resales around $370K, one of the highest resale price points in the directory for Ohio. Central Ohio is also within reach of the Mid-Ohio Sports Car Course in Lexington, which helps explain the concentration of motorsports-oriented projects here.

Southwest Ohio: Cincinnati and Dayton (5 communities, 93 units)

Southwest Ohio has fewer units but some of the most upscale product in the state. Torque Motor Suites Blue Ash is selling 30 units from $399,900, the highest published new-unit entry price in Ohio, and Torque Motor Suites Oakley in Cincinnati is an established project with units from $300K. Garage Condos at Mason Pointe is selling 9 units, Forrer Pointe in Kettering, near Dayton, is selling 54 units, and Park Place Storage Condominiums in Lebanon is planned.

Three takeaways:

  • Ohio has two price tiers. Storage condos on the lake and in Northeast Ohio cluster from roughly $66K to $200K. Purpose-built motor suites in Cincinnati and Columbus start around $300K to $400K.

Storage Condo vs. Luxury Motor Suite: Which Ohio Product Fits You?

Because Ohio has so much of both, it is worth spelling out the differences.

Storage condos

Projects such as Catawba, NorthCoast, West Bay, Store-With-Us, Buckeye, Tin Man and Mega Storage are classic storage condominiums. They are typically metal or masonry buildings with large overhead doors, a concrete slab, basic electrical service and simple common areas. They suit boats, RVs, trailers, multiple vehicles, tools and business inventory. HOA dues tend to be modest because there are few shared amenities. Buyers who want to add heat, insulation, a mezzanine or a lift should confirm what the declaration allows and what the building's electrical service can support.

Motor suites and car clubs

Torque Motor Suites (Blue Ash and Oakley), Horsepower Farms, Hudson Pointe, The Motor Enclave New Albany, Executive Storage Estates and Resolute Motorsports Club are aimed at car enthusiasts. Expect more architectural design, finished interiors, climate control options, mezzanine lounges, upgraded lighting and floors, and sometimes shared clubhouse or event space. These carry higher purchase prices and often higher dues, but they also come with a social element that storage condos usually lack: cars and coffee events, owner gatherings and a community of fellow enthusiasts.

Neither is better in the abstract. A boat owner on Catawba Island and a collector in Blue Ash are solving different problems. The best buy is the one that matches how you will actually use the space.

Ohio-Specific Buying Considerations

Seasonal demand on the lake

Lake Erie communities are busiest in spring (launch season) and fall (haul-out season). If you are buying a resale unit in Port Clinton or Marblehead, inventory and competition can shift with the boating calendar. Ask sellers and the association about typical turnover, and keep an eye on the directory listings during the off-season when motivated sellers sometimes list.

Winter, heat and moisture

Ohio winters are cold and damp, especially near the lake. For car storage, ask whether units are insulated, whether heaters or dehumidifiers are permitted, and how the association handles snow removal. A cold, unconditioned unit can be fine for a winterized boat but less ideal for a collector car you want to drive on a sunny February weekend.

Phased and planned projects

Five Ohio communities are planned, and Catawba's sixth phase is under construction. Buying pre-construction can secure a better location or price, but read the purchase agreement carefully: how deposits are held, what the completion deadline is, what specifications are guaranteed, and what remedies you have if the project changes. Our buying guide walks through the full checklist.

Business use and mixed communities

Several Ohio projects (Avon Lake Office & Storage Condos, Island Landings Business & Storage Condos, Biz Caves) explicitly accommodate business users. That can be a plus if you run a business, or a consideration if you want a quiet weekend retreat. Read the use restrictions and talk to existing owners.

Renting your unit

With a large owner base and seasonal boating demand, renting out a storage condo can be viable in some Ohio markets, but rental rules vary by declaration. Check before you buy if rental income is part of your plan, and see our renting guide for what to look for.

How to Shop for an Ohio Car Condo

  • Decide on product type: storage condo for value and flexibility, motor suite for amenities and community.
  • Pick a region: the lake for boating, Northeast Ohio for value near Cleveland, Columbus for new luxury projects, Cincinnati for high-end suites.
  • Use the data: start with the full Ohio directory and the interactive map to shortlist by status, units and pricing.
  • Request documents: declaration, bylaws, rules, budget, reserves and recent minutes from every finalist.
  • Compare comps: in large lake associations, ask for the last several closed sales of similar-size units.
