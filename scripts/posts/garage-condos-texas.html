Texas is the most brand-driven garage condo market in America. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 42 communities across the state, roughly 450 known units in the communities that publish counts, and half of the Dallas-Fort Worth market alone belongs to a single developer: Garages of America, with 15 Texas locations in the directory. Layer on a new wave of ultra-luxury product, Maranello in Plano at roughly $650 per square foot, Toy Cave in Houston at $839,000 to $865,000, and the T11 Car Condos at Circuit of The Americas starting at $1.25 million, and you get a market that runs from practical $192,500 shop space in Cypress to seven-figure trackside trophies in Austin.

If you are searching "garage condos Texas," "car condos Dallas," or "garage condos Houston," this guide maps the whole state: who builds where, what each metro actually costs, and where the 2026 to 2027 pipeline is adding inventory. Every community mentioned is listed in the full Texas directory, and you can browse all 42 on the interactive map.

Half the state's communities sit in DFW, and that is no accident. North Texas combines enormous collector car wealth, cheap developable land along its tollway corridors, and HOA-heavy suburban housing that leaves serious collections with nowhere to live at home. Houston's dozen communities skew toward the northwest suburbs, Cypress and Katy, plus a genuine luxury tier inside the city. Austin's five communities include the most expensive car condos in Texas, and San Antonio is a small but active market with its flagship project currently under construction.

What Is Driving Texas Demand

Three structural forces keep absorbing new Texas supply as fast as developers can build it. The first is garage math: the typical master-planned Texas suburb caps out at a three car garage, HOAs prohibit visible RV, trailer, and race car storage, and the metros keep adding high-income households who collect vehicles anyway. The second is business utility. A deeded garage condo is commercial-zoned real estate an owner can hold in an LLC, depreciate, and in many communities use for a low-traffic business, detailing, brokerage inventory, content production, which residential garages legally cannot host. The third is motorsports. Between COTA's Formula 1 weekend, MotorSport Ranch, MSR Houston, and G2 Motorsports Park, Texas has more active trackside garage condo inventory than any state in the directory except Arizona, and track communities historically hold value because the land next to a racetrack is finite by definition.

Dallas-Fort Worth: 21 Communities, One Dominant Brand

The Garages of America footprint

You cannot talk about DFW garage condos without starting with Garages of America, the developer that effectively invented the metro's market (it originally built the concept locally as Garages of Texas before rebranding for national expansion). The directory lists 13 Garages of America communities in Dallas-Fort Worth: Allen, Carrollton, Carrollton II, Colleyville, Dallas, Frisco, Frisco II, Firewheel in Garland, Plano, Roanoke, and Rockwall in the metro, plus Katy, The Woodlands, and Towne Lake down in greater Houston and Lakeway outside Austin, for 15 Texas locations in all.

Most DFW Garages of America properties are established and sold out, which means they trade resale. The clearest public benchmark in the directory is Garages of America Roanoke, where resales run about $475,000. Two locations, Carrollton II and Frisco II, are actively selling new units, so buyers who want the brand without hunting resales still have a direct path.

For buyers, the practical effect of this footprint is standardization: comparable construction, comparable HOA structures, and resale comps across a dozen locations. That makes DFW the easiest Texas market to value, a point we expand on in the investment guide.

Plano's luxury cluster

Plano has become the luxury capital of the Texas garage condo world, with three very different products inside one suburb:

  • Maranello Luxury Auto Garages: 79 units selling at roughly $650 per square foot, which works out to about $650,000 to over $1.3 million per unit. Maranello is less a storage facility than a car-centric social campus, built around club amenities and event space, and it is the price ceiling for non-trackside product in Texas.
  • The Garage x Dennis Collins: a 38 unit community selling now, carrying the name of one of the most recognizable figures in the North Texas collector scene. Celebrity-branded garage communities are a genuinely new category, and this is Texas's flagship example.
  • Garages of America Plano: the established, resale-driven original that anchors the submarket.

The rest of North Texas

Beyond Plano and the Garages of America network, DFW depth keeps growing. Trailblazer Garages Las Colinas in Irving is selling 42 units from about $500,000, with a second Trailblazer community in presales in McKinney. Texas Autohaus is selling in Dallas, Luxury Garages in Lake Dallas is selling its Phase II, Storage Caves Forney has reservations open east of Dallas, and LuxeLocker McKinney is coming soon with the brand's buy-or-rent model. North of the metro in Anna, G2 Motorsports Park Garage Condos sells units from $495,000 attached to a motorsports park, and southwest of Fort Worth, MotorSport Ranch in Cresson is the metro's long-established track community, where garage condos trade resale alongside a private road course membership scene.

Houston: Value in Cypress, Luxury in the Loop

Houston's 12 communities sort cleanly into two stories.

The first is the northwest value corridor. Cypress alone holds four communities: Garage Werks selling from $192,500, Showcase Luxury Garages selling 24 units from about $225,000, Garages of America Towne Lake selling now, and LuxeLocker Cypress under construction with buy-or-rent flexibility. Those two published entry points, $192,500 and roughly $225,000, are the lowest new-construction numbers in the directory's entire Texas dataset, which makes Cypress the best answer in the state to "garage condo under $250K." Katy adds Garages of America Katy (established) and Republic Luxury Garage Condos (selling), The Woodlands has its established Garages of America location, and Friendswood's Garage Ultimate covers the southeast side.

The second story is a fast-forming luxury tier inside Houston proper. Toy Cave Luxury Garages is selling 56 units at $839,000 to $865,000, pricing that would have been unthinkable in Houston a few years ago and now sits within range of Plano's Maranello. Paddock Car Condos, a boutique 9 unit community, and Garage Ultimate Spring Branch round out the in-city options. And 45 minutes south in Angleton, MSR Houston sells garages at the metro's road course for owners who want their storage at the track.

Austin: COTA and the Seven-Figure Ceiling

Austin has only five tracked communities, but it owns the top of the Texas market. T11 Car Condos at Circuit of The Americas is selling units from $1.25 million, trackside real estate at the only Formula 1 venue in the United States, named for the circuit's Turn 11. Nothing else in Texas carries a published starting price anywhere near it, and nothing else offers F1-weekend proximity as a property amenity.

Below that ceiling, Garages of the Americas in Del Valle, minutes from COTA, is selling its Phase III within a 152 unit community, the largest single community count in the Texas directory. Garages of America Lakeway serves the Hill Country lake crowd on the west side, while LuxeLocker has two buy-or-rent locations coming to the metro in Austin and Round Rock. For a market of its size, Austin's pipeline is thin relative to demand, which is exactly the setup that has historically pushed garage condo resale values upward.

San Antonio: Small Market, Real Pipeline

San Antonio's four communities are easy to summarize. Dominion Motor Club, the city's established name, is resale only. Its successor, Dominion Motor Club 2.0, is under construction with 50 units and is taking $5,000 reservations now, the most concrete pre-construction opportunity in South Texas. Dream Garage Condos and Shavano Garage Condos are both actively selling. Buyers here get something DFW rarely offers anymore: first pick of new inventory in a market with almost no existing supply.

How to Buy in Texas Right Now

The status mix favors buyers more than in most states. Of the 42 tracked Texas communities, 22 are actively selling new or developer units, 13 are established complexes that trade resale, 2 are under construction, 4 are planned, and only 1 is strictly resale only. In plain terms: more than half of Texas communities will sell you a unit directly today.

A few strategy notes by buyer type:

  • DFW brand buyers: If you want Garages of America, Carrollton II and Frisco II are the two direct-sale doors still open; everything else means watching resales, where Roanoke's ~$475,000 comps are your pricing anchor. Resale inventory in sold-out communities surfaces irregularly, so set alerts on the Texas directory rather than checking sporadically.
  • Value buyers: Cypress is your market. Garage Werks from $192,500 and Showcase from about $225,000 are the lowest published new-build entries in the state, and LuxeLocker Cypress will add a rent-first option when it opens.
  • Track-focused buyers: Texas offers four distinct motorsports plays at four price levels: MSR Houston garages in Angleton, MotorSport Ranch Cresson resales, G2 Motorsports Park in Anna from $495,000, and T11 at COTA from $1.25 million. Each pairs the real estate with a very different track culture, so visit before you wire a deposit.
  • Pre-construction buyers: Dominion Motor Club 2.0 in San Antonio ($5,000 reservation, 50 units) and Storage Caves Forney (reservations open) are the live opportunities; LuxeLocker Austin, Round Rock, and McKinney are coming behind them. Read our buying guide on deposit protections and delivery-date contingencies before reserving anything unbuilt.

Whichever metro you shop, start with the interactive map to see how communities cluster along the Dallas North Tollway, US-290 in Cypress, and the COTA corridor. And if you own a unit in a sold-out community, especially a Garages of America property or anything in Plano, scarcity is working in your favor: list your unit on the directory to reach buyers who are already searching that exact community.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do garage condos cost in Texas?

Published pricing in the AutomotiveCondos.com directory runs from $192,500 at Garage Werks in Cypress to $1.25 million and up at T11 Car Condos at COTA in Austin. The practical middle of the market sits near $475,000 to $500,000, where Garages of America Roanoke resales, G2 Motorsports Park in Anna, and Trailblazer Las Colinas all cluster, while Plano's Maranello runs about $650 per square foot ($650,000 to over $1.3 million) and Houston's Toy Cave sells at $839,000 to $865,000.

Who is the biggest garage condo developer in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Garages of America, by a wide margin. The directory lists 15 Texas locations, 12 of them in the DFW metro including Allen, Carrollton, Colleyville, Dallas, Frisco, Garland (Firewheel), Plano, Roanoke, and Rockwall. Most locations are established and sold out, trading only on the resale market, with Carrollton II and Frisco II currently selling new units.

What is the most expensive car condo community in Texas?

T11 Car Condos at Circuit of The Americas in Austin, selling from $1.25 million. The premium buys trackside ownership at the only Formula 1 circuit in the United States. Among non-trackside communities, the ceiling is Maranello Luxury Auto Garages in Plano, where 79 units sell at roughly $650 per square foot, topping out above $1.3 million.

Are there trackside garage condos in Texas?

Yes, four tracked options: T11 at COTA in Austin (from $1.25M), G2 Motorsports Park Garage Condos in Anna north of DFW (from $495K), MotorSport Ranch in Cresson southwest of Fort Worth (established, resales), and MSR Houston garages in Angleton (selling). They span every tier from club-racer practical to F1-adjacent trophy property.

Can I rent a garage condo in Texas instead of buying?

Increasingly, yes. LuxeLocker is bringing its buy-or-rent model to four Texas locations: Cypress (under construction) plus Austin, Round Rock, and McKinney (planned). In sold-out communities, individual owners also lease units privately from time to time. Renting is a sensible first step if you want to test a location before buying, though owners, not renters, capture the appreciation that sold-out DFW communities have shown.
