Where you buy a garage condo matters as much as what you buy. Supply, pricing, association quality, and resale liquidity vary enormously by state, and until now there has been no national dataset to compare them. This ranking is built on hard numbers: the AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 273 garage condo communities across 42 states, the most complete national inventory of the asset class we know of. Below we rank the best states for garage condos in 2026 by community count, name the flagship developments in each tier with real price points, and, just as useful for investors and developers, call out the markets that are conspicuously underbuilt relative to their demand profile.

How We Ranked the States

The primary metric is simple and honest: the number of distinct garage condo communities in each state in our directory. Community count is a proxy for three things buyers care about: choice (more communities means more unit types and price points), validation (developers build where demand has proven out), and resale liquidity (deep markets trade; thin markets sit). We layer on qualitative factors, flagship communities, price dispersion, and growth pipeline, in the tier write-ups. One caveat: count measures supply, not value. A state with five communities and waitlists can be a better buy than a state with twenty and soft pricing. Use the tiers as a map, then drill into each state page for the community-level picture.

Tier 1: Florida, the Garage Condo Capital

No state is close. Florida accounts for roughly 100 of the 273 communities in the directory, more than a third of the national market, spanning every format from basic drive-up bays to full motorsports country clubs. The drivers are obvious once listed: year-round car culture, salt air and hurricanes that make conditioned storage a necessity rather than a luxury, enormous boat and RV ownership, strict residential HOAs that ban driveway storage, and a decade of wealth migration into the state.

The flagship examples bracket the market. The Motor Enclave in Tampa, a motorsports community with its own performance track, has seen resales ranging from the high-$300Ks for entry garages to about $2.1 million for large built-out trophy units, a spread that tells you how much build-out and location-within-community matter. At the accessible end of the enthusiast spectrum, Sebring Motor Garages, adjacent to one of America’s most storied race circuits, has offered units in the $395K–$525K range. Between those poles sit dozens of workaday communities across Tampa Bay, Orlando, South Florida, Sarasota, Naples, and the Panhandle serving collectors, boaters, and small businesses at lower price points. Florida is also where appreciation has been best documented: recorded sales data per county records show the median garage condo resale price up roughly 87% from 2021 to 2026. The flip side of Tier 1 status: more new supply is under construction in Florida than anywhere else, so buyers should study each submarket’s pipeline, not just its past.

Tier 2: Texas and Arizona, Scale and Institutional Platforms

Texas ranks second with 31 communities, and its defining feature is platform-scale development. Garages of America alone operates 14 locations in the state, the closest thing the industry has to a statewide branded network, which standardizes quality and gives buyers a reference point for pricing across metros. At the trophy end, COTA T11 at Circuit of The Americas in Austin lists trackside units from $1.25 million, Texas’s answer to the motorsports-club product. Dallas–Fort Worth and Houston carry the bulk of the inventory, with Austin and San Antonio growing fast behind them.

Arizona is third with 21 communities, nearly all concentrated in metro Phoenix and Scottsdale, arguably the densest single-metro garage condo market in America relative to population. The anchor operator is Toy Barn, with 9 Phoenix-area locations, alongside storage-condo players like LuxeLocker expanding across the Southwest. The demand story writes itself: Barrett-Jackson and the Scottsdale auction ecosystem, extreme summer heat that makes climate control mandatory, massive snowbird and RV populations, and one of the country’s deepest collector communities. For buyers, Texas and Arizona offer something Florida’s fragmented market sometimes lacks, repeat developers with track records, which de-risks new-construction purchases considerably.

Tier 3: The Established Regionals

Below the big three sits a band of states with real, functioning markets, enough communities for genuine choice and comparables, but still thin enough that good units move fast.

  • North Carolina (10) and Georgia (10): the Southeast growth story beyond Florida. Charlotte’s motorsports economy (NASCAR country) and Atlanta’s sheer scale anchor steady development, with South Carolina (8) riding the same migration wave along the coast and Upstate.
  • Colorado (6) and Washington (6): mountain-west and Pacific Northwest markets where harsh weather, outdoor-toy ownership (RVs, boats, snowmobiles), and tech wealth support premium product. Washington’s market notably spans both Seattle and Spokane, where lease rates from roughly $1,250/month show secondary metros can carry premium pricing.
  • Illinois (6): the Chicago metro’s collector base is enormous, and communities like Iron Gate Motor Condos in Naperville, one of the Midwest’s original and most emulated luxury garage communities, prove the model works in cold climates, where heated space is non-negotiable.
  • Minnesota (5): home of AutoMotorPlex, a pioneering brand whose Chanhassen flagship helped define the clubhouse-community format nationally.
  • Alaska (5): the tier’s surprise. Five communities in a state of ~730,000 people is the highest per-capita supply in the country, driven by brutal winters and the highest per-capita ownership of planes, boats, and off-road machines in America. Proof the product is weather-driven as much as wealth-driven.

Worth watching just outside the top ten: Michigan, where M1 Concourse in Pontiac, 87 acres with a private performance track in the cradle of the auto industry, has posted resales from roughly $399K, and Wisconsin, where Road America’s orbit and Milwaukee’s collector scene support a small but committed market.

The Underbuilt Markets: Where Supply Hasn’t Caught Demand

The most interesting part of a national dataset is what is missing. Several markets have every demand ingredient, wealth, car culture, climate pressure, restrictive residential HOAs, yet little or no garage condo supply in the directory:

  • Las Vegas. A major collector-car auction market, extreme heat, a motorsports economy, and explosive in-migration, yet supply remains thin relative to Phoenix, a smaller leap away with 21 communities. The gap is striking.
  • Inland Southern California. Coastal California land costs make garage condos nearly impossible to pencil, but the Inland Empire, Temecula, and the high desert combine cheaper land with one of the largest car-enthusiast populations on Earth. The handful of existing projects sell well; the market could absorb far more.
  • Nashville. Tennessee’s migration boom, no state income tax, and a fast-growing wealth base mirror the conditions that built Florida’s market a decade ago, with a fraction of the supply.
  • The Northeast outside New Jersey. Boston, Connecticut’s Gold Coast, Westchester, and Philadelphia hold some of America’s densest collector wealth and its worst winter storage problem, yet the directory shows remarkably little purpose-built supply outside New Jersey’s handful of communities. Land cost and zoning friction explain some of it; the first developers to crack these metros will find waiting buyers.

For buyers in these markets, the practical takeaway is to move quickly when a project does break ground, underbuilt markets absorb fast. For developers, this is the expansion map.

How to Use This Ranking

Three closing rules. First, buy the community, not the state: a well-run association in a Tier 3 state beats a troubled one in Florida, so vet the declaration, dues history, and reserves everywhere (our buying guide covers the checklist, and the cost guide covers what you’ll pay beyond the sticker). Second, in Tier 1 and 2 markets, study the new-construction pipeline before paying peak resale pricing. Third, in thin markets, liquidity is the risk, plan longer hold periods and buy the unit type with the broadest buyer pool. Browse every community by state at our state directory or visually on the national map, and if you know a community we have not catalogued, submit it, the dataset gets better with every listing.
