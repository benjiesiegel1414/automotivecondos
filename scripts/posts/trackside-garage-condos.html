A trackside garage condo is a privately held garage that sits on the grounds of a real racetrack, road course or motorsports country club, or close enough that you can idle from your door to the paddock. It is the car enthusiast's version of a golf course home: instead of a fairway out back, you get a pit lane. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 558 car condo and garage condo communities nationwide, and after checking every one of them against track maps, club websites and local reporting, we count 33 communities in 21 states that are genuinely tied to a racing circuit. Twenty nine sit at or inside a track. Four more are track adjacent: they are near a famous circuit but do not have their own pavement.

This guide lists every one of them, explains how ownership actually works at each type of facility, and covers the questions that matter before you write a deposit check: what the membership costs on top of the garage, when you can actually drive, how noise rules work, and how easy these units are to resell. If you are new to the category, start with our car condo buying guide, then come back here. You can also see every trackside community in context on the interactive map.

What Makes a Garage Condo "Trackside"

A regular car condo is a deeded garage unit in a secured building, usually in a suburban business park near the homes of the people who use it. It is about storage, a place to hang out, and sometimes a mezzanine lounge. A trackside garage condo adds the one thing a suburban unit can never offer: a place to actually use the car at speed, legally, with corner workers, timing and a run off area.

The term gets stretched, so we used a strict test. A community counts as trackside in this guide only if it meets one of two conditions:

  • On track: the garages are on the property of an operating or permitted road course, motorsports park or private motor club, and owners or members can drive from the garage to the circuit.
  • Track adjacent: the garages are a short drive from a named circuit and are marketed and used by people who run at that track, but the community has no pavement of its own.

Anything that only borrows racing vocabulary (Paddock, Pit Row, Victory Lane, Grand Prix) without a circuit failed the test. More on that in the sidebar below.

The three ownership models

Trackside garages are sold in three very different ways, and the difference matters more than the square footage:

  • Deeded garage condo. You buy the unit outright, get a deed, pay HOA or condo association dues and property tax, and can finance it like other commercial or specialty real estate. Atlanta Motorsports Park, G2 Motorsports Park, M1 Concourse and Sebring Motor Garages all sell deeded units. Owning the garage does not always include driving rights; at many clubs a separate membership is required to use the track.
  • Garage lease or rental. The track keeps ownership and rents garages by the day, month or year. NCM Motorsports Park in Kentucky and Oregon Raceway Park both work this way. You build no equity, but your exit is a notice letter instead of a listing agreement.
  • Membership with garage rights. At private motor clubs such as Monticello Motor Club, Ten Tenths Motor Club and The Thermal Club, the membership is the core product and the garage, villa or storage space attaches to it. Most of these clubs charge an initiation fee plus annual or monthly dues, and some require you to own real estate on site to join at all.

Initiation fees and dues vary widely and change often. Where a club has published numbers or reputable outlets have reported them, we cite them below. Everywhere else, assume a membership cost on top of the garage and ask for the full fee schedule in writing.

Florida: The Busiest Trackside Market

Florida has more trackside garage projects than any other state, and most of them are new. Four of the five are either planned or actively selling, which tells you where developer money is going. Florida also has the deepest pool of regular car condo buyers, so if you want the full statewide picture, read our Florida car condo guide or browse Florida garage condos for sale.

Circuit Florida Car Condos (Auburndale)

Circuit Florida sits along I-4 between Tampa and Orlando. Its centerpiece is a 1.7 mile track with 14 turns and about 40 feet of elevation, a real achievement in flat central Florida. The CEO has compared the concept to a golf country club with the track standing in for the course. Our directory lists 75 units, status Selling, pricing on inquiry.

P1 Motor Club (Port St. Lucie)

P1 is the most ambitious project in the state. According to The Shop, the plan includes a 3.0 mile Treasure Coast Circuit and a 4.5 mile, 22 turn P1 Circuit, plus rally, drift and karting tracks, for more than nine miles of surface. Members buy a charter license, and garage townhomes and trackside homes are optional rather than required. The directory lists P1 as Planned, pricing on inquiry. This is a membership first model: budget for the license as a separate cost from any garage.

Hyper Club (Naples Racing Resort)

Hyper Club proposes to convert a former vehicle proving ground in eastern Collier County into a private racing community. WGCU reports that the site already has a 2.1 mile straightaway and a 1.2 mile handling course, and that conservation groups oppose the plan because of its location near the Florida Panther National Wildlife Refuge. Status: Planned, pre-construction. Treat any reservation here as a bet on permitting.

Emerald Coast Motor Club (DeFuniak Springs)

This Panhandle project is promoted as a motorsport country club built around a proposed 3.25 mile track with a karting circuit and member facilities. It is Planned and prices on inquiry. It would give the 30A and Destin crowd a home circuit, but nothing is built yet.

Sebring Motor Garages (Sebring, track adjacent)

Sebring Motor Garages is not on the track, but it is the closest thing to it: less than a mile south of Sebring International Raceway, and it is the raceway's official motor condominium partner. IMSA reported that owners get access to 25 track days a year through a partnership with the Sebring Driving Club. Units include a bathroom, shower and mezzanine, and the zoning allows overnight occupancy. The directory lists 148 units, Selling, priced $395K to $525K.

Texas: Four Tracks, Four Very Different Models

Texas has the widest spread of trackside options, from a Formula 1 venue to a long running member club. For the broader market, see our Texas garage condo guide and current Texas listings.

T11 Car Condos at COTA (Austin)

T11 is the only car condo project on the campus of Circuit of The Americas, the country's purpose built Formula 1 circuit. Community Impact reported two level floor plans from 1,500 to 6,000 square feet, a private race viewing platform, clubhouse and pool, and discounts on COTA events. Note that the perks described are about watching and hosting rather than lapping. The directory lists T11 as Selling, from $1.25M, the highest published entry point in this guide. See more in Austin.

G2 Motorsports Park Garage Condos (Anna)

G2, north of Dallas, is a 3.1 mile private circuit founded by former racer Lou Gigliotti. When it launched, Connect CRE reported a lifetime membership of $100,000 plus $450 a month, which shows clearly how a club membership stacks on top of garage ownership. The directory lists the garages as Selling, from $495K.

MotorSport Ranch (Cresson)

Opened in 1999, MotorSport Ranch is one of the original American "country club with a road course instead of a golf course" models. Per its history, it runs a 1.7 mile east course and a 1.3 mile west course that combine into a 3.1 mile, 16 turn layout, members pay an initiation fee plus monthly dues, and the facility has around 300 garages. The directory shows it as Established, with trackside garages available as resales.

MSR Houston Garages (Angleton)

MSR Houston, south of the city, runs a 2.38 mile, 17 turn road course plus a karting track, rally course and skidpad, on a club model where members pay per session. The directory lists garages as Selling, pricing on inquiry. Houston buyers comparing it with in town storage can check the Houston city page.

Southwest and West

Apex Motor Club (Maricopa, AZ)

Apex is Arizona's private motorsports club south of Phoenix. Its first track measured 2.27 miles, and in 2025 new owner RJT Capital announced a roughly $30 million investment including 48 more garage condominiums and a premium clubhouse, alongside a second circuit. The directory lists Apex as Established with track-side garage condos. Arizona context is in our Arizona car condo guide.

The Thermal Club (Thermal, CA)

Thermal is the benchmark for the whole category. Its three courses can be combined into a 5.1 mile GT circuit, and membership is tied to owning a villa on the property, with an initiation fee and monthly dues on top. The directory lists trackside villas and garages, Established. Expect the highest carrying costs in this guide. See also our California car condo guide.

Monterey Motorsport Park (Monterey, CA, track adjacent)

Despite the name, Monterey Motorsport Park is a garage condo complex, not a circuit. It sits on Highway 68 next to Monterey Regional Airport, along the corridor that leads to WeatherTech Raceway Laguna Seca, with 86 units, ceilings up to 25 feet and a mezzanine in every unit. It is Resale only. It makes the track adjacent list because it serves the Laguna Seca and Monterey Car Week crowd, not because you can lap from your door. More in Monterey.

Spring Mountain Motor Resort Garage Condos (Pahrump, NV)

Spring Mountain, an hour west of Las Vegas, calls itself the longest road course in North America, with more than 6 miles of pavement and 50 plus configurations. Its loft condos sit above climate controlled garages, and members can lease them full time or on a timeshare basis. Status: Established, pricing on inquiry.

Oregon Raceway Park Trackside Garages (Grass Valley, OR)

ORP is the lease model in its purest form. Its published garage rental agreement lists rates of $125 a day, $550 a week or $790 a month, bans fuel storage and mechanical work inside the unit, limits garage access to normal track hours, and strongly recommends that tenants carry at least $200,000 in liability coverage naming the track as additional insured. The directory lists annual leases, Established. It is a useful reminder that rules at trackside garages can be stricter than at a suburban condo.

Dominion Lux (Woodford, VA)

"Paddock" Branding Does Not Mean Track Access

Racing words sell garages, and plenty of developers use them for buildings that are nowhere near a circuit. During our review we excluded every community that uses racing branding without a track. Examples of themed, not trackside communities include Paddock1 Premium Garage Condos in Tampa, The Paddock of Delray, Paddock Car Condos in Houston, and Winners Circle Motorsports Garages and Brickyard Motorsport Garages in Lake Havasu City. Naples Motor Condos (Race Track Rd) takes its name from a street, and GKR Paddock Club in Missouri is a car campus whose own coverage describes no on site track. These can be excellent garages. Just do not pay a trackside premium for a name.

We also checked and excluded Arcadia Motor Club in Phoenix, Dominion Motor Club in San Antonio, Garages at Crescent Ridge Ranch in Washington and Motorsport Lofts (Carefree Crossing) in Arizona, because we found no circuit attached to any of them.

What to Know Before Buying a Trackside Garage

Membership versus ownership

Ask one question first: does buying the garage give me the right to drive? At M1 Concourse, membership has historically been limited to garage owners. At P1, the membership is the product and real estate is optional. At G2, a lifetime membership was priced separately from the garages. Get the membership agreement, the fee schedule and the condo declaration, and read how they interact. If the membership can be terminated or repriced, your garage's value may move with it.

Track day rules and scheduling

Clubs control the calendar. Members usually get open lapping days, but tracks also rent the circuit to manufacturers, race schools and event organizers, and those days are closed to members. MotorSport Ranch, for example, gives members access on roughly 40 weekends a year. Ask how many member days the club actually runs, whether you can bring guests, whether you need instructor signoff before driving solo, and what run groups exist.

Noise and use restrictions

Tracks live under noise permits and court orders. Lime Rock's Sunday racing ban dates back to 1959. Read the decibel limits, quiet hours and any limits on loud race cars, and read the condo rules too. Oregon Raceway Park's rental agreement bans fuel storage, fluid changes and welding inside the unit, and many associations limit overnight stays unless the zoning allows living quarters.

Insurance

Your regular auto policy almost never covers on track driving, and most collector car policies exclude it. Track day coverage is a separate policy priced per event or per season. You will also need a property and liability policy for the garage itself. The association's master policy typically covers the building shell, not your contents, and tracks may require you to name them as additional insured.

Resale liquidity

Trackside garages have a narrower buyer pool than suburban car condos: your buyer must want that specific track and be able to afford its membership. Resales can take longer, and membership transfer rules matter. Some clubs let memberships transfer with the unit; others require the new buyer to join from scratch. For the investment case in general, read are garage condos a good investment, and for loans, see how to finance a garage condo.

Living quarters and lofts

A loft above the garage turns a track weekend from a hotel booking into a stay at home. Motorsport Park Hastings, BIR, Sebring's townhomes and Spring Mountain all offer some form of living space. Confirm what the zoning actually permits: "mezzanine lounge" and "overnight occupancy" are not the same thing.

Transport and trailer storage

If you tow a race car, check the garage door height, the turning radius in the driveway, and whether trailers can be stored on site between events. NJMP units were built with doors large enough for a motorsports trailer. If you plan to drive your car to the track on the street, check the commute instead.

Rent before you buy

Several tracks rent garages by the day or month, which is the cheapest way to learn whether you will actually use one. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a trackside garage condo?

A trackside garage condo is a privately owned or leased garage on the grounds of a racetrack, road course or private motorsports club, or very close to one, so the owner can store a car and drive it on the circuit. AutomotiveCondos.com counts 33 such communities in 21 states as of November 2026.

Do I need a club membership to use the track if I buy a trackside garage?

Often, yes. At many motor clubs the garage and the driving rights are separate: you buy or lease the garage and pay an initiation fee and dues for track access. At some clubs membership is limited to garage owners, and at others the membership is the main product and real estate is optional. Always read the membership agreement alongside the condo documents.

How much do race track garage condos cost?

Among the 8 trackside communities that publish a price, entry points range from $225K at Atlanta Motorsports Park to $1.25M at T11 Car Condos at COTA, with most between about $378K and $495K. Most clubs price on inquiry, and membership fees and dues are usually extra.

Which state has the most trackside garage condo communities?

Florida leads with 5 trackside communities, followed by Texas with 4 and North Carolina with 3. California, Michigan and Virginia have 2 each, and 15 other states have one.

Does my car insurance cover driving at a track day?

Usually not. Standard auto and most collector car policies exclude on track use, so you need separate track day coverage for the car, plus a property and liability policy for the garage. Some tracks also require garage tenants to carry liability coverage naming the track as additional insured.
