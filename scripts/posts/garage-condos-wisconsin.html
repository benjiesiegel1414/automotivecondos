Wisconsin is one of the most active garage condo markets in the Midwest, and it is not hard to see why. Long winters, a deep boating and powersports culture, a world-famous road course at Elkhart Lake and a housing stock full of two-car garages that were never built for a boat, a pontoon, a snowmobile trailer and a weekend car all push owners toward private, deeded storage. The AutomotiveCondos.com directory tracks 26 garage condo and car condo communities in Wisconsin, with 1,003 known units across the 24 projects that publish a unit count.

What makes Wisconsin different from Sun Belt markets is price. This is one of the few states where a buyer can still find new garage condo units published under $100K, and where most published entry prices sit between roughly $100K and $200K. This guide covers where the 26 communities are, what they cost, which ones are selling today, and how Wisconsin-specific issues (cold-weather construction, boat and RV storage, heated units and HOA rules) should shape your decision. Every number below comes from the full Wisconsin directory, which you can also browse on the interactive map.

Wisconsin Garage Condos at a Glance

  • 26 communities across 25 cities, from Somerset and Hudson on the Minnesota border to Sturgeon Bay and Egg Harbor in Door County.
  • 1,003 known units across the 24 communities that disclose unit counts. Only two (Trackside Road Garage Condos in Elkhart Lake and 3rd Space Garages in Verona) do not publish a count.
  • 16 communities are actively selling from the developer, 6 trade on resale only and 4 are planned.

If you are searching for garage condos in Wisconsin, the headline is simple: supply is real, it is spread across the state, and most of it is aimed at practical storage buyers rather than six-figure-plus luxury clubs. That gives you more choice and more negotiating room than in most states.

Where Wisconsin's Garage Condos Are

We grouped the 26 Wisconsin communities in the directory into five regions. The split is unusually even, which is one reason Wisconsin works well for buyers: you rarely have to drive more than an hour to reach a project.

Northeast Wisconsin: Green Bay, Fox Valley, Door County and Elkhart Lake (8 communities)

The northeast is the largest region by community count. Around Green Bay, Woodfield Court Motor Condos in Suamico is the standout: 101 units with published pricing from $171K to $360K, the widest range and the highest top price in the state. Nearby Hobart Storage Condominiums offers 44 units from $98K to $151K, and Overdrive Motor Condos in De Pere is a planned 22-unit project. In the Fox Valley, Fox Valley Man Caves and She Sheds in Neenah is selling 37 units on an inquire basis.

Door County is its own market. The peninsula sits between the bay of Green Bay and Lake Michigan, and it is one of the Midwest's best-known boating and summer home destinations. That creates steady demand for secure storage for boats, pontoons, golf carts and seasonal vehicles from owners who cannot or do not want to keep them at a cottage. The directory lists three Door County projects: Indigo Storage Condos in Egg Harbor (10 units, about $170K to $200K), plus two in Sturgeon Bay, Whitetail Storage Condominium (63 units, resale only) and Boat House Motor Condos (15 units, selling). See all garage condos in Sturgeon Bay on the city page.

Finally, Trackside Road Garage Condos in Elkhart Lake trades on resale only, with units near Road America. For track-day drivers and race fans, a garage within reach of one of the country's most famous road courses is a very different product from a boat storage unit, and resales there tend to attract a specific buyer.

Madison and South Central Wisconsin (8 communities)

Dane County and the surrounding towns match the northeast with eight communities. Springfield Storage Condominiums in Dane is selling 60 units, 3rd Space Garages is selling in Verona, and two projects are planned: Freedom Storage Shops in Windsor (25 units) and Third Car Garage in Sauk City (26 units). Farther out, Shop Space Wisconsin in Reedsburg is a planned 12-unit project near the Wisconsin Dells area, and Xtra Space in Lake Mills is selling 12 units.

South of Madison, two projects offer some of the lowest published prices in the state. Newville Garage Condominium in Edgerton (Newville), near Lake Koshkonong, reports 2026 sales from $120K to $148K across 58 units, and B&J Storage Condos in Milton lists 45 units at about $98K to $100K.

Milwaukee Metro (4 communities)

P2 Auto Vault & Business Condos in Grafton, north of Milwaukee, is the largest project in Wisconsin at 133 units, with published prices from $148K to $311K. The "business condo" part of the name matters: projects like this often appeal to contractors and small business owners as well as collectors, so read the declaration to understand what uses are allowed next to your unit. The other three Milwaukee-area communities trade on resale only: Wisconsin Storage Condos (New Berlin) with 47 units, Lake Park Storage Condos in Big Bend with 42 units, and Saturn 5 Storage Condos in Waterford with 23 units.

St. Croix Valley and Western Wisconsin (5 communities)

The St. Croix Valley across the river from the Twin Cities is one of the most interesting garage condo clusters in the Upper Midwest. Many buyers here are Minnesota commuters and lake owners who want storage close to the interstate. Troy Condo Garages in Hudson lists 30 units at about $200K, the highest published entry point in the state. NR Garage Condos in New Richmond is selling 60 units at about $159K, North End Condo Garages in North Hudson is selling 18 units, and St Croix Garage Condos in Somerset is selling 24 units. Park Place Storage Condominiums (Houlton), a 70-unit project, trades on resale only with resales around $122K.

The Northwoods (1 community)

Wisconsin Storage Condos (Eagle River) is selling 26 units in the heart of snowmobile and lake country. Eagle River sits on a chain of lakes and is a well-known snowmobiling destination, so expect buyers who want to keep sleds, trailers, ATVs and boats in one place near a seasonal cabin.

How Big Are Wisconsin's Projects?

Wisconsin projects range from tiny (10 units at Indigo in Egg Harbor) to substantial (133 units at P2 in Grafton). Larger projects usually mean a more established HOA, more resale activity and better price discovery. Smaller projects can mean a quieter, more private setting, but also fewer comparable sales when you go to resell.

Who Buys Garage Condos in Wisconsin

The Wisconsin buyer pool is broader than the classic "collector car" stereotype. Based on how projects describe themselves and where they are located, four groups dominate:

  • Boat and pontoon owners. Door County, the Lake Koshkonong area near Newville, the Northwoods lakes and the St. Croix River all generate demand for indoor boat storage. A deeded garage condo replaces years of paying for marina winter storage and gives you access to your boat whenever you want it.
  • Snowmobile, ATV and RV owners. Multiple machines plus trailers quickly outgrow a home garage. Eagle River and the western Wisconsin projects attract owners who want everything in one secure place.
  • Car enthusiasts and track drivers. Projects branded as "motor condos" (Woodfield Court, Overdrive, Boat House) and the resale units near Road America serve collectors who want a heated space to store, detail and work on cars, often with a mezzanine lounge.
  • Contractors and small businesses. Business-friendly projects such as P2 Auto Vault & Business Condos and the "shop" style projects (Shop Space Wisconsin, Freedom Storage Shops) appeal to tradespeople who need secure space for tools, inventory and work vehicles.

Knowing which group dominates a project matters. A community full of contractors will have more weekday traffic and different HOA priorities than one full of weekend collectors.

Wisconsin-Specific Buying Considerations

Heat, insulation and winter use

In Wisconsin, an unheated garage condo is a very different product from a heated one. If you plan to work on vehicles in January, keep batteries healthy, store fluids or simply use the space year-round, confirm whether the building is insulated, whether units are plumbed for gas or set up for electric heat, and who pays for it. Ask for the declaration's rules on heater types and for a typical winter utility bill from an existing owner.

Snow removal and access

Snow plowing is one of the biggest line items in a Wisconsin garage condo HOA budget, and it directly affects whether you can actually get your car or trailer out after a storm. Ask how the association contracts for plowing, what the trigger depth is, whether the drive aisles are wide enough to swing a trailer when snow is piled, and whether there are reserve funds for asphalt repair after freeze-thaw seasons.

Door size and ceiling height

Boat and RV owners should measure first and shop second. Ask for exact overhead door width and height, ceiling clear height and unit depth. Lift buyers need enough clear height for a two-post or four-post lift plus the vehicle on top; mezzanine buyers need to know how much height the platform takes away.

Use restrictions

Most Wisconsin declarations prohibit living in a unit and restrict hazardous materials and certain commercial uses. Some projects explicitly welcome business use; others limit it. Rules on renting units out also vary, so read the declaration before you assume you can lease the space. Our renting guide covers what to look for.

Property taxes, insurance and HOA dues

A garage condo is real property in Wisconsin, so you will receive a property tax bill. You will also pay HOA dues and carry your own contents and vehicle coverage, since the association's master policy generally covers the building shell only. Ask each project for its current budget, the dues on the specific unit you are considering and the most recent tax bill for a comparable unit. Buying New vs. Resale in Wisconsin

Wisconsin gives you both options in meaningful numbers. Sixteen communities are selling from the developer, which means you can often choose your unit location, door orientation and options. Six communities trade on resale only, including some of the largest established projects in the Milwaukee area, the St. Croix Valley and Door County.

Resale buyers benefit from seeing the finished building, a functioning HOA with a real budget, and recent closing prices. New construction buyers benefit from customization and sometimes pre-construction pricing, but take on schedule and completion risk. For the four planned Wisconsin projects (Overdrive in De Pere, Shop Space Wisconsin in Reedsburg, Third Car Garage in Sauk City and Freedom Storage Shops in Windsor), ask about deposit handling, escrow, construction timeline and what happens if the project is delayed.

How to Shop for a Wisconsin Garage Condo

  • Define your use. Boat, cars, sleds, business or a mix. This determines door size, depth, heat and whether a business-friendly project fits.
  • Pick a region. Use the interactive map to find the projects within a realistic drive of where you live or where your boat or cabin is.
  • Shortlist by status. Selling projects for new units, resale-only projects for established communities and faster possession.
  • Request documents. Declaration, bylaws, rules, budget, reserve study and recent minutes.
  • Compare total cost of ownership. Price plus taxes, dues, utilities and insurance, not just the sticker.
