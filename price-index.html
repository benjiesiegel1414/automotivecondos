Methodology

The index uses the lowest published price for each community in our directory: the developer's "from" price for new and pre-construction projects, and the lowest recent asking or sold price for resale-only communities. Rentals, deposits and per-square-foot quotes are left out of the medians. We use the median rather than the average because a handful of $1M+ suites would pull an average far above what most buyers pay. States need at least four priced communities to be ranked. Prices come from developer sites, MLS and commercial listings, and local reporting. The index is rebuilt monthly and each month is saved as a snapshot, so changes over time reflect both price moves and new communities entering the data. Frequently asked questions

How much does a car condo cost in the US?

As of November 2026, the median entry price across the 245 car condo communities with published pricing in our directory is $256K. Half of communities start below that and half above it. Entry prices range from under $50K for small storage-style units to more than $1M for luxury and trackside suites.

Which state has the most expensive car condos?

Among states with at least four priced communities, California has the highest median entry price at $731K.

Which state has the cheapest car condos?

Iowa has the lowest median entry price at $115K among states with at least four priced communities.

Are new car condos more expensive than resales?

New and pre-construction communities in our data have a median starting price of $253K (186 communities), versus $275K for resale-only listings (59 communities). Newer projects tend to be larger and more amenitized, which explains much of the gap.

How often is the Car Condo Price Index updated?

Monthly. Each update is saved as a dated snapshot, so the index builds a price history for every state over time.
